# Chapter 5: Preference, Ability, Behavior, and Identity

"I'm shy, so I'm not suited for sales."

When you ask someone who says this about their past experiences, something curious happens.

At a university cultural festival, they visited numerous shops to gather sponsorship. At their part-time job, they would proactively speak up when new hires were struggling. At events in their favorite field, they could talk with strangers for an hour.

Yet the person still says:

"But fundamentally, I'm an I."

The person is not lying. They prefer solitude and tire easily from continuous social interaction. That is also true.

However, "preferring to be alone" and "lacking the ability to influence others" are mixed together in a single sentence.

When we think about ourselves, we tend to mix at least four different things.

**Preference, Ability, Action, Identity.**

Simply separating these four makes the type much lighter.

| Layer | Question to Verify |
|---|---|
| Preference | What feels more natural? |
| Ability | What can actually be done? |
| Action | What was done in this specific instance? |
| Identity | How is this experience woven into the story of "me"? |

> When the four are mixed, personality becomes destiny.

## "Like" and "Can Do" Are on Different Axes

First, preference.

By "preference," we do not mean just feelings of like or dislike. It refers to tendencies such as where attention naturally goes first, what is used initially when thinking, and doing the same thing with relatively less fatigue.

Some people feel more natural to think before speaking; others find their thoughts clarify while talking. Some want to grasp the big picture first; others prefer to accumulate verifiable facts. Some want to decide early; others want to keep options open.

Preferences have value.

Knowing your natural direction reduces unnecessary drain. It becomes easier to choose environments where you excel, and you can avoid treating differences between people as moral issues. Instead of labeling someone "messy" or another person "cold," you might consider that their focus and order of judgment simply differ.

However, preference does not directly indicate ability.

A person who prefers tea over coffee is not necessarily unable to brew coffee. A person who uses their right hand well is not incapable of holding anything with their left hand.

Of course, psychological preferences are not as simple as beverages. Still, "what you choose often" and "what you can do well" are different questions.

Official MBTI theory is also clear on this point. The type does not guarantee ability, intelligence, skills, or success. It opposes using types to determine a person's suitability for hiring or selection.[Note 1]

Yet in everyday conversation, we almost unconsciously leap from preference to ability.

- "Because I'm an I, I'm bad at presentations."
- "Because I'm an F, I'm weak with numbers."
- "Because I'm a P, I'm not suited for management."
- "Because I'm an S, I lack creativity."

What is happening here is not an explanation by four letters. We are inferring unmeasured abilities from the four letters.

## Ability Does Not Mean It Is Used Every Time

Next, let's separate ability and action.

Ability refers to how well one can do something. Action refers to what was actually done in a specific situation.

These two are not the same.

A person with high explanatory ability may speak roughly when they lack time. A person who can empathize may not be able to listen when someone is angry. A person who can plan may postpone less important matters.

Conversely, actions lacking confidence can be accomplished splendidly when compelled by necessity.

In other words, a single action does not reveal all of a person’s abilities. Nor does one personality-test result determine future behavior.

Mixing these makes the type a convenient circular argument.

"I didn't speak because I'm an I."

The fact that they did not speak becomes evidence that they are an I. And being an I becomes the reason for not speaking next time.

But is there another explanation?

They were not fully prepared. The boss interrupted them. They had no interest in the agenda. They had been laughed at before. It takes time to choose words because it is not their native language. They are not feeling well. Or they judged that there was no need to speak.

Even the same silence has different reasons.

The word "type" can illuminate one of these. However, when we start explaining everything with just that, we stop looking at the situation.

## Identity Is an “Edited Story,” Not a Fact

The fourth is identity.

There is no need to overcomplicate the word "identity." It is simply how you tell the story of yourself: "This is who I am."

We do not remember everything with equal intensity every day. We retain events that feel like us and label mismatching events as exceptions.

A person who thinks, "I am a planner," feels days where they organized their schedule neatly as being true to themselves. A day where they impulsively changed plans and had fun might be explained as, "That day was special."

A person who thinks, "I am someone who goes along with others," remembers scenes where they accepted requests well. Days where they firmly refused might be labeled as exceptions: "The other party was just too harsh."

Neither is a lie. Identity is not a warehouse of facts; it is a story that selects facts, connects them, and attaches meaning.

The four letters speed up this editing process.

After knowing the result, we review the past.

"By the way, I've been reading books alone since elementary school. So I'm really an I."

"I always make schedules on trips. So I'm really a J."

As matching memories gather, the type gains more truthfulness. Meanwhile, actions that do not fit are placed outside the story as mood, work, the other person, or exceptions.

This is not just a problem with the four letters. Humans always do this to feel like a cohesive self.

Therefore, what is needed is not scolding oneself to "don't believe in types."

It is about returning counterexamples to the story.

"I lean toward I, and yet I have influenced others in necessary situations."

"I value F, and yet I have used strict standards to protect relationships."

Adding just the latter sentence widens the identity slightly.

## Does Personality Remain Unchanged?

Reading this far brings another question.

"Even if specific actions can change, isn't personality itself unchangeable?"

Personality has stable parts; a person who was introverted yesterday will not become a completely different person the next morning. However, being stable is not the same as being unchangeable.

A review of 207 studies tracking changes in personality traits showed certain changes in measured values during an average intervention period of about 24 weeks.[Note 2] In another digital intervention targeting 1,523 general adults, self-assessment changed in the desired direction, and small changes in others' assessments were also reported.[Note 3]

However, these are not proofs that "one can become any personality if they wish." Changes in self-assessment are not completely identical to life changes, nor do these studies investigate whether MBTI letters change.

Still, at least two extreme beliefs can be let go.

One is the belief that "humans are completely free and can become anything with effort."

The other is the belief that "personality is fixed, so habits other than usual actions cannot be acquired."

We are not blank sheets. We are not stones either.

There are differences in ease of change. There are costs involved. Environmental support is also necessary. Nevertheless, there is room to practice actions and increase easier responses.

What this book promises is not a change in type.

It is "in situations where you could only see one option before, a second action becomes visible."

## If You Accept Yourself, Can You No Longer Grow?

The appeal of type talk also lies in the fact that it relieves self-blame.

"It's not laziness; maybe I'm a P and need margin."

"It's not being cold; maybe as a T, I prioritize problem-solving."

People saved by such reinterpretations might feel like they are losing the self-acceptance they gained when told to "practice on the opposite side too."

However, accepting is not the same as refusing to change.

In four experiments targeting people who faced failures or weaknesses, those encouraged toward self-compassion showed higher motivation to change weaknesses, motivation to make amends for mistakes, and time spent learning after failure compared to those who blamed themselves or tried to boost self-esteem.[Note 4]

These are short-term experiments, so the same results do not apply to everyone or every goal. Still, it becomes clear that being kind to oneself does not necessarily stop growth.

Rather, if change is treated as punishment for "the current self is insufficient," a single failure becomes a verdict on the whole self.

"After all, I'm this type, so I couldn't do it."

On the other hand, if considered a small experiment, failure becomes information.

The time to speak was too late. The prepared sentence was too long. Too much of one's own interpretation was shared before asking the other person. It was supposed to end in 15 minutes, but from the start aimed for completion.

What needs to change is not one's value, but the next way of doing things.

## Trying to Separate into Four Boxes

Here, let's actually decompose a single sentence.

As an example, write this:

> I'm a P, so I can't meet deadlines.

Separate this into four parts.

### 1 Preference

It feels more natural to keep options open before deciding. When new information comes in, there is a desire to change the plan.

### 2 Ability

To what extent are you able to work backward from the deadline, estimate workload, and create intermediate versions? Are there past scenes where this was done?

### 3 Behavior

When did you start this time? What happened while time passed? Who did you show it to midway? At what point did you realize you would be late?

### 4 Identity

What story do you hold: "I can't do anything unless pushed" or "Plans are not like me"?

When separated, places for intervention increase.

Do not deny your preference; practice your ability. Change the action this time. Add counterexamples to your identity.

It is not necessary to "stop being a P."

For example, while keeping the preference to want options open, you can create a "minimum version to show" three days before the deadline. You can also leave margin for new information.

This is not acting like the opposite type. It is a clever way of handling one's own preferences and real-world demands simultaneously.

## Turn Type from Noun to Verb

The four letters are nouns.

I, N, F, P. Short, easy to remember, and they summarize yourself.

However, what changes life is usually verbs.

Speak. Listen. Verify. Wait. Decide. Refuse. Try. Return. Rest.

The question "What kind of person am I?" is important. However, if the answer increases while actions decrease, it might be time to change the question.

"What will I do in the next instance?"

It is not necessary to discard the type. Add one verb after the noun.

"I lean toward I, so I think alone before meetings. And then, speak only one sentence in the first 10 minutes."

"I value F, so I consider the impact on the other person. And then, verbalize judgment criteria so as not to leave ambiguous expectations."

Preferences become preparation that helps action.

The four letters are not reasons to close doors, but clues to think about how to open them.

## Small Experiment: Separate “That’s Just Who I Am” into Four Layers

Recently, select a sentence that may have narrowed your own possibilities.

> I'm ______, so I can't / am not suited for ______.

Write the following four things next.

1. **Preference**: Which way do I naturally choose?
2. **Ability**: What can actually be done, and what has not yet been practiced? What are past counterexamples?
3. **Action**: In the most recent instance, what was done / not done? What was in the situation?
4. **Identity**: Using this event, how am I telling the story of who I am?

Finally, select one verb that can be done without changing the type.

> If a scene like ______ comes up, I will ______.

For example,

> If it seems like 10 minutes will pass before being asked for an opinion in the next meeting, I will speak the prepared sentence.

Big declarations are not needed.

Do it once and see what happens. Record not only your fatigue but also the information gained, the other person's reaction, and work results.

As a result, you may choose the current way again. That is also not a failure. Previously, it was chosen automatically because of the type. Now, it is chosen after seeing necessity and cost. There is a difference.

Preferences are the starting point.

Abilities are the current location.

Actions are the next step.

And identity is how to retell that journey.

When the four are separated, the future closed within the four letters begins to move again.
