# Workbook: Add One Choice in 30 Days

The goal of this work is not to change your type.

The objective is that, thirty days from now, in one specific situation, you can choose an action other than your usual reaction. That is the goal.

You do not need to complete every exercise. Instead, we will handle a single real-life problem over 30 days on a small scale.

Choose one of the following two methods based on how much time you have available for recording.

## Lite Version: Four Questions in One Minute Per Day

If keeping a detailed daily record feels burdensome, answer only the following four questions.

1. What happened today?
2. How did I automatically try to respond?
3. Did I try another action?
4. What actually happened?

Recording "I did not try" is also valid. Review the reasons why you could not try and whether the action can be made even smaller once a week.

You do not need to do this every day. Writing it down only on days when the target situation occurs is sufficient.

## Deep Version: Comparing Predictions and Results Over 30 Days

Use the following Deep Version if the same difficulty keeps recurring and you want to examine conditions and recovery costs in detail.

### 0 Choose One Situation to Address

Select a situation that meets the following criteria:

- It has occurred at least twice within this month.
- You feel that you would like to change the outcome slightly.
- A single small experiment will not cause damage that cannot be recovered from regarding work, income, health, safety, or important relationships.
- The change is not one forced by others.

Examples of such situations include:

- Having thoughts during a meeting but not speaking until after it ends.
- Immediately offering solutions while listening to someone else speak.
- Changing plans every time a new idea arises, resulting in nothing being completed.
- Being unable to change even when encountering information that does not fit the initial plan.
- Concluding "This job is not suited to my type" from a single failure.

### The Situation I Will Address

> When ＿＿＿＿ happens, I usually ＿＿＿＿. As a result, ＿＿＿＿ tends to occur.

### Week 1: Notice

During the first week, do not try to change anything.

Whenever the same situation occurs, record the following five items:

1. **Signal**: What happened immediately before?
2. **Action**: What did I do in observable words?
3. **Physical Reaction**: How were tension, fatigue, speed, and heaviness?
4. **Short-term Benefit**: What did that action protect or preserve?
5. **Later Cost**: What remained after a few minutes, hours, or days?

Translate evaluative terms such as "passive," "indecisive," or "cold" into descriptions of action.

- Passive → A question arose, but I did not speak during the meeting.
- Indecisive → Continued comparing candidates until two days before the application deadline.
- Cold → Explained the procedure for solving the problem before listening to the other person's predicament.

Success in Week 1 is not about change.

It is successful if you can state "when, under what conditions, and what I was doing" even once.

### Week 2: Reconsider

In the second week, investigate what is missing from that situation.

Do not answer according to your type. Look at all of the following eight items:

| Direction | Question |
|---|---|
| External | What information should be obtained from the person involved, the situation on the ground, or other people? |
| Internal | Is there time to think independently, separate from reactions from those around me? |
| Facts | Did I distinguish between what was confirmed and what was inferred? |
| Possibilities | Are there explanations, methods, or futures that have not yet been considered? |
| Principles | What criteria are used for decision-making. Can the same explanation apply to the same conditions? |
| Impact | Whose interests are affected by what. Did I ask the person involved? |
| Convergence | What needs to be decided and by when? |
| Margin | What should remain undecided, and what information will be used to review it? |

Select one item that is most lacking in this situation from among the eight.

> Is what is missing in this situation information/action regarding ＿＿＿＿?

This is not a conclusion but a hypothesis.

### Week 3: Try Small

In the third week, turn the missing element into a single action.

A good experiment satisfies the following five conditions:

- **Small**: Can be reduced to within 15 minutes, one sentence, one person, or one instance.
- **Observable**: Can be judged as whether it was executed, rather than "trying hard."
- **Has a Signal**: Determine when and under what circumstances to execute it.
- **Reversible**: Can change the approach for the next time after seeing the results.
- **Wanted by Me**: Not an action based on type theory or forced by others.

### Turn It Into If-Then

> If the signal ＿＿＿＿ occurs, I will do ＿＿＿＿ once.

Examples:

- If the chairperson asks for opinions on the first agenda item, I will read the prepared sentence.
- If a solution occurs to me while I am listening, ask, “Do you want me to keep listening, or do you want to solve this together?” before offering it.
- If a new idea comes to mind, advance the current minimum version by 15 minutes and then note it down.
- If information contrary to the plan arrives, add it to a comparison table for the next review day.

For the first attempt, make the difficulty as low as 3–4 out of 10 points.

If execution is not successful, do not conclude that you lack willpower. Instead, verify whether the action was too large or if the signal was ambiguous.

### Week 4: Reweave

In the fourth week, do not score yourself based on "did it work" or "did not work." Instead, weave the results into the breadth of your actions.

### Record of One Experiment

| Item | Record |
|---|---|
| Situation | When, where, with whom, and what happened? |
| Prediction | What did I think would happen if I took the action? |
| Execution | Did I perform the small decided action? |
| Facts | What was actually seen or heard? |
| Experience | How were mood, tension, fatigue, and recovery? |
| Others' Reactions | What direct reactions could be heard. Did you distinguish them from inferences? |
| Difference | How did reality differ from the prediction? |
| Next Time | What should remain, be made smaller, or be changed? |

Even if it succeeds once, verify again under what conditions that action can be used.

If it does not succeed, do not use it as proof of your type.

- Was the action too large?
- Did the way of communicating with the other person not match?
- Was the hypothesis about the power needed for that situation incorrect?
- Were non-personality conditions such as risk, authority, time, or health status overlooked?

Use that information to reorganize the next attempt.

### Reflection After 30 Days

Answer the following six questions:

1. Under what conditions is my usual reaction likely to occur?
2. What was my usual reaction protecting?
3. Under what conditions was the opposite action required?
4. Among the new actions, what can be used again next time?
5. What should not be used, delegated to others, or for which support should be sought?
6. How can the sentence "I cannot do this because I am ＿＿＿＿" be rephrased now?

Finally, complete the following sentence:

> I usually tend to ＿＿＿＿. That carries the strength of ＿＿＿＿. At the same time, when ＿＿＿＿, I can also try ＿＿＿＿ once.

That sentence is not a new type name.

It is one additional option you have acquired in reality.
