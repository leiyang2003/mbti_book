# Chapter 8: S and N — Seeing Facts and Seeing Possibilities

Yuto's proposals always make people feel excited.

Why is this necessary now? What kind of world can we build in three years? How does it differ from other services? The story is strong and makes the listeners excited.

At the end of a meeting, someone asks:

"So, what will we do during the first week?"

When that question arises, Yuto begins talking about new possibilities again. The answer has not yet been found.

There are also opposite scenes.

We know past figures, customer requests, and current procedures in detail. Yet when asked, “What if these conditions change?” or “What needs might our current customers be unable to articulate?”, possibilities without a track record can feel unrealistic.

The power to see facts and the power to see possibilities.

S and N serve as entry points for considering these two aspects.

Relying on only one side leads to being tripped up by reality or drifting away from it.

## S Is Not Just Detail-Oriented; N Is Not a Mark of Genius

In four-letter culture, S is sometimes depicted as practical and detail-oriented, while N is portrayed as imaginative and intelligent.

This depiction already introduces a value judgment.

In reality, accurately observing concrete facts can be a high-level ability, just as grasping invisible relationships and possibilities can be.

Whether one prefers S or N, observation can be careless or it can be creative.

The question of S/N broadly addresses which direction attention tends to focus when receiving information. It is not a ranking of intelligence or creativity.

"S lacks originality because they are an S" or "N cannot handle practical work" are leaps from preference to ability.[2]

## From N to S — Bringing Concepts Down to Earth Today

People who see possibilities articulate what does not yet exist.

They read patterns from events before them, find other meanings, connect distant ideas, and draw pictures of the future.

However, possibilities are infinite.

Every time a new connection is seen, direction can change. Explanations grow larger, and initiation becomes delayed. Treating details as "something someone else will do later" leaves behind unfinished concepts that no one can execute.

The strength on the S side is not to shrink dreams.

It exists to create points where dreams touch reality.

### The Smallest Step: Three Facts and One Step Today

When a concept or worry becomes large, divide a piece of paper into two.

On the left, write three facts that can be confirmed right now.

- Numbers, dates/times, and words actually spoken by others.
- Resources that already exist.
- Today's constraints.

On the right, write one step achievable within 24 hours.

Do not finish all research; instead, ask one person. Do not complete the service; instead, show a paper prototype. Do not decide on life direction; instead, talk for 20 minutes with someone doing a candidate job.

Facts do not deny imagination.

Facts tell imagination where to touch down next.

Yuto wrote down three facts available at hand at the time. There were two potential customers who listened to him. The one task they both struggled with was common. On paper, he could create the flow for that task by the end of the day.

He did not create a finished product; instead, he showed a paper prototype to one person. The other party paid little attention to the functions Yuto thought were important and stopped their hand at another place.

This was not a failure. It was the moment his concept received a response from reality for the first time.

## From S to N — Past Facts Alone Cannot Reveal Change

People who value concrete information support reality.

They remember what actually happened, reproduce procedures, and maintain quality. They allocate necessary time and costs to plans that are merely words.

However, facts are information about the past and present.

They do not directly tell us about a future where conditions have changed.

"No precedent" is not the same as "cannot be done." "Customers do not request it" does not eliminate possibilities that have not yet been imagined.

Furthermore, there are multiple explanations for a single event.

A colleague did not reply. Were they busy? Opposed? Did they not read it? Was the question unclear? The fact is simply "no reply."

The strength on the N side is not an escape into fantasy from reality.

It is the power to assign one or more meanings that are not yet confirmed to facts.

### The Smallest Step: Three Alternative Explanations

When about to jump straight from a single fact to a conclusion, write three alternative explanations.

> Fact that occurred: __________.
> Explanation A: __________.
> Explanation B: __________.
> Explanation C: __________.

All three do not need to be correct.

The purpose is not to believe the first explanation is the fact itself.

In a proposal, consider three uses other than the current one. In a career, consider three paths to use current skills in another industry.

Research suggests that divergent idea generation and creativity can be trained to some extent. However, an increase in ideas on a creativity test does not necessarily translate into real-world innovation.[1]

Therefore, in the end, return ideas to facts.

Which alternative explanation will we verify with what?

## Do Not Write Facts and Interpretations in the Same Column

There is a simple way to balance one-sidedness between S and N.

Separate facts from interpretations.

### Fact

Things that can be roughly confirmed among recordings, records, and observers.

"Two questions were raised about my proposal in the meeting."

### Interpretation

The meaning given to the fact.

"Everyone opposes my idea."

### Possibility

A development that has not yet occurred but is conceivable.

"If I answer the questions, the proposal might improve."

### Next Confirmation

Action that brings interpretations and possibilities into contact with reality.

"Ask the questioner about their single biggest concern."

When these four are separated, S and N do not compete.

Facts do not close off imagination, and imagination does not ignore facts; a cycle is created.

## Learning from the Past vs. Closing Off to the Past

Regardless of type, we rely on experience.

Experience becomes wisdom to avoid repeating the same mistakes.

On the other hand, the stronger the experience, the more "it was like this before" dominates new situations.

To learn from the past, two questions are needed.

"What conditions were the same as last time?"

"What conditions have changed from last time?"

If only looking at the same conditions, possibilities disappear. If only looking at changed conditions, lessons disappear.

The reciprocation between S and N is not about choosing between past or future.

It is to consider a future that does not yet exist while holding onto the concreteness of the past.

## Small Experiment: Three Facts, Three Alternative Explanations

Choose one problem you are currently wrestling with.

1. Write down three facts that can be confirmed.
2. Write down three alternative explanations for those facts.
3. Decide on one action within 24 hours to verify the most important alternative explanation.

If facts are insufficient, gather them.

If there is only one possibility, increase it.

Finally, act.

The power to see reality and the power to see beyond reality.

When these two reciprocate, concepts do not end as dreams, and facts do not end merely as a reproduction of yesterday.
