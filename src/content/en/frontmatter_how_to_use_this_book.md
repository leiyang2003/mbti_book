# How to Use This Book

This book is not about determining your "true type."

Nor is it a guide to memorizing the personalities of the sixteen types in greater detail.

What this book addresses is the "after" of the four-letter code.

You read the descriptions of the types and felt, "That's right." You found names for your differences, and things became a little easier. Yet problems at work, in relationships, and regarding career paths remain.

In such moments, how can you use the four letters not only to explain yourself but also to select your next action?

That is the question this book asks.

> From "I am this kind of person" to "I can also do this."

## You Can Read This Even Without Knowing Your Four Letters

A confirmed type is not required for the exercises in this book.

Anyone who has never taken a personality test, whose results have changed frequently, or who is not satisfied with their own type can read and use this book.

What we observe is not the letters themselves, but real-life situations.

- What is happening?
- What am I doing automatically?
- What is that action protecting?
- What does repeating that same action obscure?
- What small thing can I try next time?

These five questions can be asked even without knowing your type.

## Reading in Order Will Show You Why We Practice

In Part One, we examine the security provided by the four letters and the process by which labels become identity.

In Part Two, we return to Jung's typology's confrontation with "one-sidedness" of the mind, distinguishing between preference, ability, behavior, and identity.

In Part Three, we practice both sides of E/I, S/N, T/F, and J/P not as types, but as capacities to use when needed.

In Part Four, we summarize these into the four steps: "Notice—Reconsider—Try Small—Reweave," and apply them to work, relationships, and career paths.

You can also begin using only the exercises from the start. However, if you are pushing yourself with the thought that "I must use the opposite side more," please read Chapter 6 first. Choosing not to practice, choosing to rest, or choosing to seek help from others are all valid options.

## Writing in This Book: One at a Time Is Enough

You do not need to complete every exercise in each chapter.

Please select one situation you are currently struggling with. A single record can be completed in just a few minutes.

Do not score your personality.

What we observe is whether you could choose this once, what happened, and how to adjust for the next time.

## About the Characters Appearing in This Book

The experiences of consulting and venture investment discussed in the Introduction are drawn from the author's own life.

On the other hand, the three characters—Maki, Hayato, and Ayumi—are "composite cases" combining multiple everyday scenes with typical difficulties. They do not reproduce the experiences of specific real people.

Their changes are not data proving the effectiveness of this book's method, but narratives for thinking abstract ideas through real-life situations.

## What This Book Does Not Address

This book is not a substitute for psychological testing, medical care, or psychotherapy.

We do not consider severe anxiety, depression, trauma responses, burnout, harassment, violence, or suffering due to discrimination as problems that can be solved simply by "practicing the opposite side of your type."

If a situation has a major impact on your safety, health, or livelihood, do not try to explain it using the four letters. Instead, use appropriate professional institutions and counseling resources.

You do not need to let go of the four letters.

Simply, do not make the answer provided by the four letters your final answer.
