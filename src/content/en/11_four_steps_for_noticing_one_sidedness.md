# Chapter 11: Four Steps for Noticing One-Sidedness

So far, we have looked at four pairs of opposites.

Connecting and stepping back to think.

Seeing facts and seeing possibilities.

Following logic and not leaving people behind.

Deciding and keeping things open.

However, real-world problems rarely arise in such neat, single categories.

A meeting right before a deadline where opinions clash. New information arrives from outside, forcing a change in plans. While listening to a partner's anxiety, you cannot lower quality standards.

All four dimensions—E/I, S/N, T/F, and J/P—are involved.

In such moments, there is no time to open a type table and check each of the eight directions one by one.

What is needed is a short procedure that can be carried into any situation.

In this chapter, we summarize it as four questions:

**Notice. Reconsider. Try Small. Reweave.**

In this book, we call this the **Four-Step Choice Practice**.

This is not a new personality assessment.

It is a method for inserting one choice between automatic reaction and reality.

> It’s not about changing your personality. It’s about adding another choice.

## 1 Notice — What am I doing automatically right now?

The first step is not to do the opposite action.

It is to observe your current behavior concretely, without judgment.

Saying "I'm a P, so it's always bad" does not tell you what to change.

But saying "In work with no external deadline, I continue gathering materials and leave two days remaining without showing anyone anything" makes the location of the behavior visible.

When noticing, turn nouns into verbs.

- Introverted → Even when a question arises, I do not speak it aloud until after the meeting.
- Emotional → When the other person seems dissatisfied, I change the standards I set.
- Careless → Before starting work, I do not confirm the criteria for completion.
- Inflexible → Even when new information arrives, I do not change the original schedule.

Furthermore, add conditions.

Who are you with? Do you have enough time? Are you in a hurry? Are you tired? Is it a safe setting or one where you are being evaluated?

Behavior does not occur in a vacuum.

By writing down the conditions as well, the explanation shifts from "I am this kind of person" (a permanent description) to "Under these conditions, I tend to react this way" (an observation).

### A Sentence for Noticing

> In situations like __________, I usually __________.

## 2 Reconsider — What is missing in this situation?

Once the automatic reaction becomes visible, do not immediately go to the opposite side; instead, look at the situation itself.

Often, your usual way of doing things is correct.

There is no need to force yourself to speak to others if you need to think alone. In a decision requiring sufficient facts, continuing to increase possibilities can be dangerous. In emergencies, there are times when clear instructions are needed rather than listening to everyone's emotions for a long time.

The question is not "Did I use the opposite side?"

It is "Is important information from this situation included in my judgment?"

Check all eight directions.

### Outer and Inner

- Is there information or reaction that should be obtained from outside?
- Do I need to step back and think on my own?

### Facts and Possibilities

- What are the verifiable facts?
- Have I considered other interpretations or futures?

### Principles and Impact

- Are my judgment criteria consistent?
- Who is affected and how? Have I confirmed this with them?

### Convergence and Margin

- What must be decided now to move forward?
- What should still be kept open?

You do not need to include all of these with equal weight.

The mix you need differs between an emergency and a new business initiative for next year.

What matters is not to mistake the information you usually notice for the whole problem.

### A Sentence for Reconsidering

> My usual way of doing things tends to overlook __________. This time, __________ might still be missing.

## 3 Try Small — What is the smallest action I can take next time?

"Be more flexible," "Increase empathy," "Look at details."

These are noble goals as objectives, but they do not tell you what to do next.

Make actions small, concrete, and observable.

- Speak one sentence in the first 10 minutes of a meeting.
- Ask one question without guessing the other person's intent.
- Confirm three facts that underpin the premise of a plan.
- Create a minimum viable version in 15 minutes.
- Do not make a final decision; set a two-week trial period.

Furthermore, link it with "when."

In psychology, formulating a goal as "If situation Y occurs, then I will do X" is called an implementation intention.

Gollwitzer and Sheeran summarized 94 independent verifications and reported that implementation intentions have a moderate to large positive effect on goal achievement.[Note 1] By linking cues for the situation with actions in advance, you become more likely to notice opportunities to begin, and the burden of hesitation at the moment is reduced.

However, if-then is not magic.

It is difficult to continue pursuing goals you truly do not want simply through automated phrasing. If the action is too large or the cue is ambiguous, you cannot execute it.

Good if-then statements have three conditions:

1. The cue is specific and actually noticeable.
2. The action is small enough for you to start on your own.
3. You understand why you are doing it.

### A Sentence for Trying Small

> If __________ happens, I will __________. This is to protect __________.

## 4 Reweave — From the result, what do I add to my range of actions?

You tried an unfamiliar action. You felt nervous. You could not speak well.

Here, old stories return.

"I really can't do this because I'm an I."

To avoid making a single unpleasant experience proof of your type, look at the results separately.

### Facts That Occurred

What did you do? What did the other person say? What happened to time, quality, and the decision?

### Your Experience

How much anxiety, fatigue, or discomfort was there? What helped you recover?

### Difference from Prediction

Did what you feared beforehand happen? Were there unexpected benefits or costs?

### Next Adjustment

Do you keep the same action? Make it smaller? Change your preparation? Decide not to use it this time?

Your own sense alone is not enough, nor are external results alone.

Even if you achieve results, if it takes two days to recover each time, you need to choose which situations to use it in. Even if you think you did well yourself, if the other person feels misunderstood, adjust your method.

This double feedback rewires the action back to yourself.

It becomes experience that "this way of doing works under these conditions," rather than just acting out the opposite side.

### A Sentence for Reweaving

> In reality, __________ happened. Next time, I will keep __________ and change __________.

## Test Your Own Prophecy as a Behavioral Experiment

Self-restriction based on type includes prophecies.

"If I refuse, the relationship will break."

"If I show something unprepared, I will be seen as incapable."

"If I speak in a meeting, I will disrupt the flow."

Prophecies remain true unless tested.

In cognitive behavioral therapy, “behavioral experiments” are used to derive predictions from beliefs and test them through real-world action. While we cannot directly transfer clinical techniques into self-help, the idea of comparing predictions with results in low-risk situations is also useful in this book.[Note 2]

When framed as an experiment, the meaning of success and failure changes.

"If the relationship did not break even after refusing," that is information weakening the prophecy.

"If the other person showed dissatisfaction," that is not proof that refusing was impossible, but information about how you said it, the relationship itself, and expectations adjustment.

Do not conduct experiments in situations with high risk.

When power differences, violence, harassment, or livelihood are involved, prioritize safety and professional support.

## Do Not Measure Change by Type Scores

As you continue practicing, your personality-test results may change. They may not.

We do not use that as a success metric.

What we want to measure is change such as:

- The number of times you considered more than two choices in important situations.
- The time it takes to notice an automatic reaction and return to choice.
- The number of times you turned guesses into questions.
- The number of times you received feedback from reality even when incomplete.
- Backtracking, regret, and friction in relationships after a decision.
- Specific changes in behavior observed by people close to you.

This is more measurable and directly connected to daily life than changing your entire personality.

## Add One Choice in 30 Days

You do not need to practice all directions every day.

For the first 30 days, choose one difficulty to work on.

### Week 1: Notice

Record when and under what conditions automatic reactions occur. You do not need to change anything yet.

### Week 2: Reconsider

Each time, confirm only one piece of missing information.

### Week 3: Try Small

Try the same if-then action two or three times in low-risk situations.

### Week 4: Reweave

Compare results and recovery costs to decide which actions to keep, which to change, and which situations not to use.

The question after 30 days is not "Did I become a different person?" but "In situations where I would have automatically chosen only one before, did another choice become visible?"

Adding just one is sufficient.

## The Four-Step Choice Practice on One Page

| Step | What to Write on One Page |
|---|---|
| Situation | When, where, with whom, and what happened |
| Notice | What I automatically tried to do |
| Reconsider | What information, values, or perspectives are still missing |
| Try Small | If __________, then I will __________ |
| Reweave | Actual results, my own experience, others' reactions. Next time, what to keep and what to change |

> There is no field for writing four letters. The situation—and you, not your type—decide the action.

If these four questions become ingrained, you can increase choices even without writing them down. That is returning type to a toolbox.
