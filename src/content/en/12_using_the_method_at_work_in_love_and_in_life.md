# Chapter 12: Using the Method at Work, in Love, and in Life

The four questions appear simple enough on a quiet desk.

Notice. Reconsider. Try Small. Reweave.

However, in reality, emotions, time, and human relationships become entangled.

In this chapter, we will try them out across five generalized composite scenarios.[2]

Our goal is not to show the correct answer. Even within the same scenario, necessary actions change depending on the relationship, role, and risk involved.

We derive questions from the situation, not answers from a type.

## Scenario 1: The Team Is Divided Before the Deadline

Three days remain until the launch of a new service.

One person argues to launch as scheduled, fixing issues later. Another insists on postponement due to a critical issue that will confuse users. In the meeting, the same arguments repeat, voices rising.

### Notice

What are you doing automatically?

- Rushing to a conclusion and trying to stop opposing views.
- Continuously increasing possibilities while avoiding a decision.
- Avoiding stating the severity of the problem to protect human relationships.
- Ignoring the other person's anxiety to prove your own correctness.

### Reconsider

- **Fact:** Where are users stopping? How often? How long does it take to fix?
- **Possibility:** What are the options for limited release, reducing features, or narrowing the target audience?
- **Principle:** What is the minimum standard for whether to launch or not?
- **Impact:** What happens to users, the team, and clients if we delay versus if we launch?
- **Convergence:** What decision needs to be made today?
- **Margin:** What can be changed after the launch?

### Try Small

Stop "for or against" for a moment. Spend 30 minutes comparing three options against the same criteria. Identify and verify just one largest missing fact that prevents judgment.

### Reweave

After the decision, review not only the result but also at which stage opposing views were raised. Next time, establish launch criteria and review dates upfront.

Here, J/P alone or T/F alone is insufficient.

Filling in missing information moves the problem more than simply matching four letters.

## Scenario 2: The Partner Says, "I Want to Be Heard"

The other person is talking about dissatisfaction at work.

You are hearing the same problem repeatedly. A solution seems obvious. You end up answering, "Then just tell your boss," or "Just change jobs."

The other person falls silent and says, "That's enough."

### Notice

When you hear a problem, you immediately move toward cause and solution.

### Reconsider

What does the other person need right now: resolution, organization, empathy, or rest? You are guessing without asking them.

### Try Small

> "Do you want to think about methods together now, or should I just listen until the end first?"

After listening to the end, summarize the content in one sentence and confirm if it matches.

### Reweave

Look not only at whether the other person has calmed down but also at whether you have been enduring things unilaterally on your own.

If continuing to listen is difficult, communicate boundaries.

> "I want to hear because it's important. But I can focus for another 15 minutes today. Is it okay to continue tomorrow?"

Empathy is not self-sacrifice.

Bring the other person's needs and your own limits into the same conversation.

## Scenario 3: Found a Job That "Fits This Type"

Searching reveals a list of professions suited to your type.

You feel relieved reading them, yet your current job is not on the list. You might need to change jobs. Jobs you are interested in are written as not fitting your type.

### Notice

You are trying to quickly close the anxiety of career choice with an answer from your type.

### Reconsider

- What specific hypothesis exists regarding which concrete actions fit or do not fit that profession?
- Does "fits" mean less fatigue, better results, liking it, or alignment with values?
- What differences exist in environment and role even within the same profession?

### Try Small

Turn type recommendations into three verifiable hypotheses.

> Hypothesis 1: Meeting new people every day means recovery cannot keep up.
> Hypothesis 2: If you can devise methods yourself, motivation continues.
> Hypothesis 3: You prefer work where you can see how results reach people.

Talk to people actually working in the field. Try short-term work. Change some conditions within your current job.

### Reweave

Record not the job title, but under which conditions you acted and how.

Type serves as an exploration tool but does not serve as a pass/fail judgment.

## Scenario 4: High Stress Makes Your Usual Strengths Too Strong

When time runs out, people tend to return to familiar reactions.

Manage more closely. Investigate more possibilities. Carry more alone. Adjust more to others' reactions. Push with logic alone.

Your usual strengths turn up the volume.

Official MBTI practice includes a model called "Grip," where the inferior function has a strong influence under stress. Some may find it helpful as words to organize experience. However, there is no need to explain all stress responses using functional order.

There are factors to consider first, such as sleep deprivation, workload, power dynamics, health status, and past experiences.

### Notice

Under stress, what quantity increases? Management, avoidance, speaking out, silence, searching, or concession.

### Reconsider

Is it really an ability issue? Or is it a matter of rest, information, number of people, deadlines, or authority?

### Try Small

Before adopting new personality behaviors, reduce one load.

- Delay one decision.
- Add one consulting partner.
- Ensure sleep.
- Make work priorities explicit with your supervisor.

Then, add just one missing piece of information.

### Reweave

Do not summarize as "a type weak to stress." Record which load, which reaction, and which support changed things.

## Scenario 5: Using Type Within a Team

Type can become a tool to start team conversations.

"I think more easily if I receive materials first."

"I want to separate the time for generating ideas from the time for deciding."

"In feedback, I want to hear both the conclusion and its impact on people."

Translating four letters into specific requests aids collaboration.

On the other hand, using type as a basis for division of labor fixes opportunities.

"I won't let an 'I' handle customer service."

"F cannot give harsh evaluations."

"P cannot hold progress management."

The person's ability, experience, wishes, and growth opportunities disappear.

If using type within a team, place five bottom lines.

### 1 Participation is Voluntary

Do not force people to disclose their results. Respect their freedom not to take an assessment.

### 2 Do Not Guess Others' Types

Do not take away the person's own words by saying, "You are absolutely T."

### 3 Do Not Make It the Deciding Factor for Hiring, Selection, or Assignment

Judge ability and suitability based on related actions, achievements, and skills.

### 4 Translate Letters into Requests

Not "because I am an 'I'," but "with materials before the meeting, I can offer better opinions."

### 5 The Person Can Correct It

Prioritize the person's own experience over type descriptions, recognizing change and exceptions.

Official MBTI ethics also oppose labeling, restriction, and selection based on type.[1]

The purpose of using type within a team is not to make it easier to slot people into roles.

It is to enable people to put their way of working into words and negotiate necessary conditions more easily.

## The Three People So Far

These three have not been reborn as people of opposite types.

Maki still prefers reading materials alone and preparing thoughts. There are days she cannot speak in meetings. However, if she notices her own information is missing from an important decision, a choice appears: to send out the prepared sentence early.

Yūto still finds multiple possibilities from one fact. Just because a minimum version is decided does not mean new ideas disappear. The difference is that he can keep everything he thought of open until the next review date without putting it all into the current version.

Ayumi still wants to help when she sees someone in trouble. Even after communicating boundaries, guilt feelings have not disappeared. However, by separating empathy from agreement and confirming current promises together, she has become able to adjust before cutting off the relationship.

Success is not always about being able to use the opposite side. It is that in a situation where only one thing was visible before, a second one became visible.

## Look Only at What Is Missing—Without Using Type

Finally, let us completely remove the four letters.

Write one real problem you are currently facing in the center.

Place eight columns around it.

- Information obtained from outside
- Judgment organized internally
- Verifiable facts
- Possibilities not yet considered
- Consistent criteria
- Impact on people and values
- What to decide now
- What to keep open

Look at the empty columns.

Decide on an action to put one piece of information there.

You can use this method without thinking about whether you are E or I, N or S.

That is important.

The four letters serve as an entrance. However, if the method cannot move without the four letters, we are still dependent on type.

Ultimately, what we want to acquire is not the ability to describe our own type in detail.

It is the ability to notice one-sidedness in a situation and compensate for what is missing.

## Small Experiment: Viewing One Problem from Eight Directions

From the five scenarios in this chapter, choose one that feels close to you or write another problem.

Fill in the eight columns and select the most empty column.

Then, add one piece of information within the next 24 hours.

- Ask one person.
- Think alone for 10 minutes.
- Verify three facts.
- Propose three alternative solutions.
- Write one criterion.
- Ask the impact from the person involved.
- Make one provisional decision.
- Leave one condition for change.

You do not need to do all of them.

Look at how the problem moves with just the most missing one.

Type is not the answer.

It helps us find the questions the answer has left out. At that point, the four letters become a tool again.
