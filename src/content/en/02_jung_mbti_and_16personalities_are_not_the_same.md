# Chapter 2: Jung, MBTI, and 16Personalities Are Not the Same

**"MBTI is not scientific."**

Articles with this headline often receive a rebuttal such as:

"That refers to free personality tests; real MBTI is different."

Then another person might say:

"But aren't they both based on Jung?"

In a single conversation, three distinct things get swapped around.

Jung's theory of psychological types. Official MBTI. The four-letter culture beginning with 16Personalities.

Because they use similar terminology, confusion is inevitable. However, arguing about what is "right" or "wrong" without distinguishing among the three leads to a loss of clarity regarding the subject matter.

The purpose of understanding these differences is not to judge authenticity versus falsehood.

It is to understand what your current map depicts and what it omits.

## Distinguishing the Three Maps, Once and For All

| | Jung | Official MBTI | 16Personalities / Four-Letter Culture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Center** | Psychological types, one-sidedness of consciousness, and compensation | Evaluation and feedback using four preference indicators | Easy-to-remember personality classifications for sharing |
| **Four Letters** | Not the modern four-letter test | Uses E/I, S/N, T/F, J/P | Uses similar four letters plus unique A/T dimensions |
| **Important Boundary** | Does not aim at classification | Does not measure ability or success; values the individual's own reflection | A continuous trait model distinct from official MBTI |

From here on, there are five things ordinary readers should remember.

## 1 What Jung Left Behind Was Not the Modern Four-Letter Test

Carl Gustav Jung published *Psychological Types* in German in 1921.

Jung first distinguished between two attitudes: extraversion and introversion. He then discussed four psychological functions: sensation, intuition, thinking, and feeling. However, extraversion does not simply mean being social, and introversion does not simply mean being shy. Rather, these refer to a broader concept regarding whether one's psychological interest tends toward external objects or toward the interior of the subject.

Jung's concern was never merely about happily categorizing people.

He sought to understand why the same event is perceived differently. When one strongly identifies with a single standpoint, what gets pushed away from consciousness? Types served as tools for considering one-sidedness in the mind.[Note 1]

Jung's writings do not contain a mechanism for measuring E/I, S/N, T/F, and J/P via questionnaires to return sixteen types. In particular, J/P was not presented by Jung as a fourth axis.

## 2 MBTI Is Not a Direct Shortening of Jung's Theory

Katharine Cook Briggs and her daughter Isabel Briggs Myers were influenced by Jung's theory of types and developed questionnaires that enabled people to understand differences and apply them to work and collaboration.[Note 2]

The two women established the four-letter code and explicitly added J/P. The concept of "type dynamics," which considers dominant and auxiliary functions from the four letters, also includes clarification and expansion by Myers, Briggs, and later theorists. Not everything was written in Jung's original texts.[Note 3]

In other words, MBTI is not "Jung himself" but rather a practical framework that developed under his inspiration.

This is not a flaw. What matters is not to read subsequent additions as if they were part of Jung's original text.

## 3 Official MBTI Does Not End with the Results Screen

Official MBTI does not assume that the questionnaire results serve as a verdict on the individual.

In official implementations in Japan, trained certified users provide feedback through which the individual reflects on the type that fits them best. It is acknowledged that the result shown by the questionnaire may differ from the type that feels right to the individual.

Furthermore, official ethics are more cautious than popular images suggest.

*   Types do not indicate ability, intelligence, or success.
*   They are not used for hiring or selection.
*   One should not determine another person's type based solely on observation.
*   Labels should not limit a person's potential.

Official MBTI theory of type development also does not teach that one should continue using only the preferred side. Maturity is explained not as abandoning preferences, but as being able to appropriately access processes even those that are not preferred when necessary.[Note 4]

Aspects overlapping with this book's perspective already exist within official theory.

Nevertheless, this book is needed because popular content from SNS—such as "relatability" posts, compatibility charts, and career rankings—often arrives before such cautious thinking.

## 4 16Personalities Is Not Official MBTI

The 16Personalities widely used by many people in Japan who say they have "taken the MBTI" is not official MBTI. The operators themselves explain that their proprietary framework, NERIS, is a continuous trait model constructed from the Big Five.[Note 5]

Continuous means that there are not just two kinds of people on the right or left; rather, many people occupy various positions in between.

Nevertheless, the results display four-letter codes and type names similar to MBTI. Categories are used because they are easy to remember and share.

Here convenience and precariousness coexist.

Two individuals scoring 49% and 51% on a given indicator are actually quite similar. Yet, because they lie on opposite sides of a boundary, they may receive different letters. Conversely, between 51% and 90% with the same letter, the strength of the tendency differs significantly.

Categories can separate similar people and place different people into the same box.

This does not render them meaningless. Like a transit map that omits street-level details for ease of use, information is omitted to make it usable.

The problem arises when the omitted information is forgotten.

## 5 The Ability to Measure Personality Is Distinct from People Falling into Sixteen Categories

Scientific debates surrounding MBTI are not a binary choice between "all nonsense" and "sixteen types proven."

Questions regarding item consistency, test-retest reliability after a time interval, correlations with other personality traits, the validity of dichotomies, and inferring function order from four letters are each separate inquiries.

Detailed research is left in the end-of-chapter notes. Here, please take away only the following five points:

1.  MBTI scales do not claim to measure nothing at all regarding people's tendencies.
2.  On the other hand, personality differences are often observed as continuous dimensions rather than two kinds of boxes.[Note 6][Note 7]
3.  It has not been proven that sixteen discrete types exist in nature as they are.
4.  Even supportive reviews have limitations regarding subjects and research design.[Note 8]
5.  From any standpoint, four letters cannot serve as grounds for determining ability, career success, or a person's value.

Use hypotheses about tendencies to update them against real-world experience.

This book stands at this middle ground.

## Knowing the Types of Maps Reveals the Limits of Expansion

There is no need to claim that 16Personalities is the same as official MBTI simply to enjoy free personality tests. There is also no need to ignore psychometric criticisms out of respect for official MBTI. Nor is there a need to assume that all later function stacks came from Jung’s original text.

Furthermore, regardless of which map is used, there is no need to determine a person's ability or future based on four letters.

Knowing the types of maps reveals how far one can expand.

In the next chapter, we will examine the process by which maps become identity. What happens when categories turn into stories, and stories preemptively decide the next action?

## Small Check: Which Map Did You Receive?

Recall what happened when you learned your four letters, and check the following points next.

1.  Which site, book, or session provided the result?
2.  Were continuous scores, such as percentages, displayed?
3.  Was there an opportunity for you to reconsider the result yourself?
4.  How much was explained regarding ability, career, and compatibility?
5.  Among those explanations, what did you verify through actual experience?

The goal is not "to retake the 'correct' test."

It is to understand what your current map omits.

No matter how precise a map may be, it is not a record of the path you have walked in your own life.
