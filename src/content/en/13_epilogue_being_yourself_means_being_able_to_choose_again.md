# Epilogue: Being Yourself Means Being Able to Choose Again

Let us return once more to the opening scene.

"MBTI, what is that?"

Answer with four letters.

"Oh, I see. That feels right."

The conversation begins.

Those four letters are still useful today.

They help you notice your own fatigue. They allow you to avoid immediately labeling someone who makes a different judgment as an "evil person." They enable you to discuss tendencies that have gone unspoken with friends.

You do not need to discard their value simply because you have finished reading this book.

However, following the four letters comes another sentence:

"I lean toward I and need time alone to think. Therefore, I will read the materials first. Then, in tomorrow's meeting, I will share one opinion within the first ten minutes."

The preference has not disappeared.

The preference has become information that prepares action.

## "Authentic" Is Not Just an Average of the Past

We tend to think of our authenticity as merely the average of what we have done well in the past.

Because I spent a lot of time alone, I am an I.

Because I went along with others, I am an F.

Because I did not decide until before the deadline, I am a P.

Past tendencies are indeed important material for understanding ourselves.

However, if our authenticity is defined solely by past averages, then everything newly learned becomes fake.

The day you first said no. The day you first shared an opinion in front of others. The day you first abandoned a plan and chose new information. The day you first grounded an ambiguous dream into today's single step.

Even if these actions feel unnatural at first, they may have been chosen based on what you hold dear.

Authenticity is not just about natural reactions.

It also appears in what you value and which responses you rechoose.

## You Do Not Need to Become a Complete Person

The ideas underlying this book include the metaphor of "full color."

If the four letters represent your strong colors, then the opposite side represents colors that are usually difficult to handle.

This metaphor is beautiful but also carries danger.

It can be interpreted as requiring you to hold all colors with equal intensity and solve any problem alone. If received that way, it becomes a new form of perfectionism.

This book does not aim for painting all colors evenly.

The goal is to know that while you have your own colors, other colors are available when the picture requires them.

You can borrow from others. You can mix in a little bit. You can decide not to use something now.

Rather than becoming a finished personality, you increase your usable options.

Once one option increases, you are no longer exactly the same person as before.

## Do Not Romanticize Pain

Sometimes, opportunities for growth lie within difficulties.

Your usual logic could not repair the relationship. Your usual flexibility could not keep a promise. Your usual consideration caused your own boundaries to disappear.

Pain signals what has been overlooked.

However, do not romanticize pain.

Not every suffering is an exercise meant to forge the opposite side.

Rest. Step away. Seek help. Put unfairness into words. Receive treatment. Use systems.

These are also actions of the self that can choose.

Being able to endure something is not maturity.

Maturity lies in observing the situation and choosing, including safety for yourself and others, values, and limits.

## What Returned in Time No One Else Could See

The change I experienced when I stopped thinking "being E-like" was my true self did not happen only in a work conference room.

It includes reading books alone. Sitting quietly in meditation. Opening a journal and writing down thoughts from the day.

These are times without results shown to others or immediate evaluations returned.

For a long time, I cultivated myself by reaching out externally, standing before people, and answering on the spot. That is an important strength acquired through work. However, as long as I continued calling that image "my true self," the joy of being alone seemed somewhat low in value.

When I actually continued reading, meditating, and journaling, I realized that from time alone, I was receiving not just recovery but also the power to think and great enjoyment.

What occurred there was not a new judgment such as "I am not E; I am truly I."

Both going out and moving with people and thinking deeply alone are actions I can hold. Depending on the day’s purpose and my condition, I can choose either kind of time.

If I had treated the "I am E" initially received as the final answer, I might still not have permitted myself the joy on the I side.

Growth is not discovering that another type was your true identity.

It is also welcoming back experiences that you had previously kept at a distance because they felt "not like me."

## Moments When Self-Understanding Becomes Strength

Even I myself did not become a different person after the experience in the introduction.

In investments, I still need to verify facts and logic and make decisions. The power to step forward and influence people is also still used within my work.

What changed was that I no longer guarded these as "this is truly me."

I began to receive others' perspectives. I left possibilities open before deciding. I read alone, thought, and wrote. I became able to choose these as my own actions depending on the necessary situation.

I do not deny that the answer “I am an ENTJ” gave me strength. Today, however, those four letters are not a sovereign. They are one tool in a toolbox.

Self-understanding has two directions.

One is the direction of making explanations more accurate.

What tendencies you hold, what fatigues you experience, what you value, and under what conditions you can exert strength.

The other is the direction of increasing freedom.

Knowing your tendencies but choosing different actions in necessary situations. Even if you return to automatic reactions, notice and return. If you overestimate others, ask the person themselves.

Even with accurate explanations, choices may decrease.

"I am this kind of person."

Self-understanding that increases freedom concludes as follows:

"I have this tendency. Then, what do I choose in this situation?"

> From “That’s just who I am” to “That’s something I can do, too.”

Knowing yourself becomes not a verdict closing the future but material for creating the future.

## The Final 30 Days

After closing the book, you do not need to remember everything.

Take just one difficulty with you.

In the first week, observe automatic reactions.

In the second week, ask about one missing element.

In the third week, try small if-then actions.

In the fourth week, look at the results and decide whether to keep them in your action repertoire.

After 30 days, it is fine not to retake a personality assessment.

Instead, ask the following:

"In situations where I could only see one choice before, have I come to see another choice?"

If the answer is "yes," that is the growth this book speaks of.

If the answer is "not yet," do not blame your personality; make your actions even smaller. Change the situation, seek help, and reconsider necessity.

The book cannot live for you.

Types cannot decide for you.

The four letters tell you where you started walking.

However, what decides how far you can go is not the four letters.

It is you who choose the next step.
