# Chapter 6: Growth Is Not About Becoming Your Opposite

So far, we have discussed not reaching the limits of your four letters.

Then, serious people tend to create new obligations for themselves.

*"Since I am an Introvert (I), I must meet more people."*

*"To correct my one-sidedness in Intuition (N), I must do all the detailed tasks as well."*

*"I cannot avoid Feeling (F) and must become someone who can speak harshly."*

Thinking you have stepped out of the box of your four letters, you now find yourself inside a new box: "Someone who can use both sides perfectly."

This is exactly what this book seeks to avoid most.

Growth is not about becoming the opposite type.

It is not about taking on everything you are bad at, nor is it about enduring unnaturalness indefinitely.

It is about being able to choose ways other than your usual approach when necessary.

## Your Strengths Are Not Something to Discard

The term "one-sidedness" carries a negative connotation.

However, expertise emerges from one-sidedness.

Because you continue to focus on details, you can find errors others miss. Because you continue to consider possibilities, you can envision plans that do not yet exist. Because you continue to pay attention to people's reactions, you can sense small changes in the atmosphere. Because you think deeply on your own, you can develop a perspective that is not swayed by trends.

If you do not fully cultivate your strengths and aim for equality from the start, you may end up going nowhere deep.

Furthermore, there is no need to be able to do everything yourself.

Teams have division of labor. When people who are strong in details, those strong in planning, those who connect relationships, and those who advance decisions collaborate, the result can be better than one person carrying everything alone.

Your energy also has limits.

Rest after meeting with many people. Organize your schedule after a period of change. Reflect alone after absorbing others' emotions.

Such recovery is not escaping growth; it is a condition to regain the state where you can choose again.

Do not interpret the phrase "transcending your type" as denying your type.

It means starting with your current point and adding paths suited to your destination.

## Four Conditions for Practicing the Opposite Side

So, when is there value in trying out a side different from your usual self?

In this book, we use four conditions.

### 1. The Same Problem Repeats Itself in Different Forms

Even if the other person changes, the relationship sours for the same reason.

Even if the work changes, the same confusion occurs before deadlines.

You come up with many plans but move on to the next one before any take shape.

Even if your usual way works well in many situations, if you are repeatedly paying a price in the same place, there may be something overlooked.

### 2. Important Values Cannot Be Protected by Your Current Way

You value harmony with others. However, because you cannot refuse requests, you become exhausted and eventually cut off the relationship.

You value freedom. However, because you make no decisions, you miss opportunities you truly wanted to pursue.

You value logic. However, truths that others cannot participate in are not implemented.

Preferences and values are not the same.

Sometimes, actions that are difficult to choose in your daily life are necessary to protect your values.

### 3. You Cannot Leave That Ability to Others Indefinitely

Division of labor is effective. However, not all important abilities can be outsourced.

Even if you are bad at management, you must manage the minimum required by your own commitments. Even if you are unfamiliar with handling emotions, you must listen to what close people are trying to convey. Even if speaking exhausts you, you must speak up in situations involving your rights.

Actions related to agency in life cannot be left entirely to those who are good at it.

### 4. You Can Try It Small and Safely

Even if growth is necessary, there is no need to make a huge bet immediately.

You do not need to stand before a hundred people without preparation to practice public speaking. You do not need to wipe out an important plan entirely to test flexibility. You do not need to start with the most powerful person to practice saying no.

Low risk, ability to return to the previous state, and ability to observe results.

The ability to try small is a condition for practice.

## If It Feels Uncomfortable, Are You Growing?

There is the phrase "step out of your comfort zone."

Indeed, unfamiliar actions can feel uncomfortable. Your heart rate rises before speaking. Guilt arises after refusing. Confirming a plan feels like losing other possibilities.

If you avoid discomfort alone, your range of action will not expand.

However, feeling uncomfortable is not proof that the action is correct.

Pain has different meanings:

*   Tension arising from unfamiliarity.
*   A signal that you are exhausted beyond your capacity limits.
*   Discomfort from acting against your own values.
*   Normal reactions to harassment or unfair environments.
*   Suffering that requires professional support, such as anxiety, depression, or trauma.

If we label all of these as “growing pains,” we may push ourselves when we should rest and adapt to environments that are unjust.

Pain might be an entrance.

However, there is not necessarily a letter on the opposite side waiting beyond it.

Rest, boundaries, collaboration, changes in systems, or consultation with specialists may be necessary.[1]

## Necessity, Risk, Reversibility, and Recovery Cost

Before trying an action different from your usual self, check these four points.

### Necessity

What is this action protecting? Is it something you desire, or is it a sense of obligation to "be someone who can do anything"?

### Risk

If you fail, what happens? Is it merely embarrassing, or does it have a major impact on your work, income, safety, or health?

### Reversibility

Can you try it small, see the result, and return? Can you create a trial version before making a final decision?

### Recovery Cost

How much rest and organization are needed after that action? Can you secure recovery time?

Looking at these four points allows you to move away from the binary choice of "don't do it because it's scary" versus "do it even though it's scary."

If necessity is high, risk is low, it is reversible, and you can recover, try it small.

If necessity is low, and risk or recovery cost is high, do not do it; share the load with someone, or change your environment.

Choosing not to act can also be a proactive choice.

## Jobs That Suit You and Jobs You Can Do

Type-specific "suitable careers" are popular.

This is because people crave answers when facing uncertain career paths.

Descriptions of types can be used as keywords for exploration.

Do you need time to concentrate alone? Is it better to have more time interacting immediately with people? Is an environment with change and freedom preferable, or one with clear roles and procedures? What motivates you: the meaning of work, impact on people, or technical difficulty?

Such questions are helpful.

However, jumping directly from type to job title eliminates information in between.

Even within "Sales," actions differ between new business development, existing customers, online, retail, and technical sales. Even within "Research," the ratio of time spent analyzing alone versus coordinating jointly differs.

Furthermore, what you are good at, what you can do, what you want to do, and what is necessary for your life may not align.

Type recommendations change conclusions into hypotheses.

Instead of "I am an Introvert (I), so I am not suited for sales," consider:
"There is a hypothesis that I tend to burn out with high-frequency new contacts. Let me verify in small steps which sales environment and to what extent this occurs."

Gather information from reality through informational interviews, short-term projects, workplace visits, or part-time work.

Experience updates hypotheses, not your four letters.[2]

## The Opposite Side Is Not a Mask That Betrays You

When you act differently than usual, you may feel it is "not the real you."

An introvert leads the discussion. A person who values harmony sets standards and refuses requests. A person who likes freedom sets deadlines.

It might feel like acting.

However, are only things you can do naturally considered your "real self"?

When you first ride a bicycle, your movements are unnatural. When you start speaking a foreign language, it feels like it is not your own voice. On the first day of becoming a parent or a manager, roles have not yet settled into your body.

Unnaturalness can be evidence that something has not yet been automated, rather than proof of being fake.

Of course, this does not mean you should adapt to any role.

Did you choose it from your own values? Is it limited to necessary situations? Can you recover? What happens to you and others if you continue?

While considering these factors, decide whether to keep the action in your repertoire of behaviors.

New actions are not masks that contradict yourself.

They are your own verbs—ones you have not yet grown used to using.

## Small Judgments: Protect, Delegate, Practice, Ask for Help

Choose one thing you think you are bad at because "it is my type."

Consider which of the following four it is closest to.

### Protect

This involves healthy preferences or recovery. Do not force a change; instead, adjust your environment.

### Delegate

It is better for the whole if you borrow others' strengths. Clarify roles and responsibilities and share them.

### Practice

It involves important values or agency, and can be tried small and safely. Decide on the next action.

### Ask for Help

The suffering or risk is too great to handle with self-help practice alone. Connect with trusted people, workplace systems, or professional support such as medical, psychological, or legal services.

You do not need to decide on just one.

They can be combined, such as "Delegate partially, but practice the minimum."

Growth is not about being able to do everything yourself.

It is about choosing what to protect, what to learn, and where to borrow strength from others.

In the next four chapters, we will use the four pairs of E/I, S/N, T/F, and J/P as entry points for considering these choices.

This is not to determine which type you are.

It is to verify whether the power of the other side is needed in the situation before your eyes.
