# Chapter 4: Jung Was More Concerned with One-Sidedness Than Classification

When people hear about type theory, they tend to think of it as a system that divides people into boxes.

Extraversion and Introversion. Sensing and Intuition. Thinking and Feeling.

However, reading Jung's *Psychological Types* reveals that types are more than just names on a classification chart; they represent the question of "why human consciousness becomes one-sided."

We cannot see everything at once.

If we examine facts in detail, we overlook other possibilities. If we follow a logical thread, we postpone the pain arising from relationships. If we respond quickly to external reactions, we miss the discomfort inside ourselves that has not yet been put into words.

To see something is also to not see something else.

> Consciousness is like a flashlight illuminating a dark room. The more brightly one area is lit, the darker other areas remain.

## Because We Have a Flashlight, We Can Act

Consciousness is not a giant camera that receives all of the world.

It selects what is needed, distinguishes between foreground and background, and directs attention toward meaningful directions. It is because we have choices that we can act.

At work, you forget surrounding sounds to focus on documents. After an argument with a partner, you worry only about their expression and cannot hear the content of what they are saying. When considering a new venture, possibilities appear one after another, while necessary procedures for today fall out of view.

In each case, the direction of the flashlight is different.

Jung believed that the orientation of consciousness inevitably becomes one-sided. He explained that as consciousness strongly advances in one direction, excluded content gathers on the unconscious side and performs a compensatory function.[1]

This "compensation" is not a mechanical switch where emotions command after overusing logic, or where the unconscious disrupts plans after over-planning.

In this book, we do not treat the other side as a mere mechanical opposite either.

Even if someone inclined toward J abandons all schedules, or someone inclined toward T stops using logic, growth will not occur. What is needed is to verify what the current light illuminates and what it leaves in darkness.

## Strengths Become "Me" the More Successful They Are

People rely on methods they can use well.

If success comes from organizing through thinking, you will use thinking again next time. If you are praised for accurately grasping and evaluating concrete facts, you will rely on sensing again next time. If you protect the situation by reading people's values and relationships, you will use that judgment again next time.

**Success. Increased frequency of use. Mastery. Becoming "myself."**

This is the natural flow of strengths developing.

However, eventually, your skilled methods become strongly identified with your ego.

"The me that can think is my true self."
"The me who understands people's feelings is valuable."
"The me who can foresee the future is truly me."

Then, the opposite side begins to appear not only as a weakness but also as something unlike oneself, of low value, and even dangerous. You may even come to view the side you do not use as someone else's flaw.

Strengths becoming blind spots is not because the strengths are wrong.

It is because you continue to point the flashlight in the same direction and forget that there is information outside that light.

## The Dark Side Is Not a Hidden "True Personality"

Jung called the most conscious and usable function the dominant function, and the least differentiated function the inferior function.

Here, "inferior" does not mean morally inferior; it refers to a position where consciousness finds it difficult to use freely and remains undifferentiated. Jung did not consider this itself pathological and acknowledged its potential for development.[2]

However, there is no need to turn the dark side into a mysterious second personality.

The fixed "eight-function stack" often seen on the internet is not a completed table left exactly as Jung did. The rules for inferring the order and orientation of functions from four letters include extensions by Myers and Briggs and later theorists, and there are debates regarding empirical evidence.[3]

In this book, we do not treat function order as a growth schedule.

We do not decide that "because your fourth function is this, you should cultivate it next." Necessary actions are chosen from the situation at hand rather than from the four letters.

What lies in the dark side is not necessarily a specific function.

It could be words from a partner you have not yet heard, facts you have not yet verified, possibilities that have been closed off, or your own discomfort before putting it into words.

These are not "higher selves," but rather information that has not yet entered judgment.

## The Purpose of Type Is Not to Decide the Box

Jung himself expressed dissatisfaction with type theory being read as a game of classifying people.

In *Collected Works* Volume 6, §986, he stated that the purpose of type theory is not to sort people into boxes, but to provide tools for organizing complex psychological material and critically examining one-sided perspectives.[4]

Of course, Jung also used classification terms; he did not completely reject classification.

What he opposed was making classification the goal itself.

Not "Which box does this person belong to?"

But rather, **"What am I overlooking?"**

In this sense, this book returns to Jung.

It returns not to modern four-letter types including J/P or later function tables, but to the purpose of using types.

From classification, to awareness of one-sidedness.
From explaining oneself, to questioning one's own perspective.

## Individuation Is Not About Making Four Letters 50/50

When discussing the development of Jungian psychology, the term "individuation" is used.

Here, this book wants to emphasize only one point.

Individuation does not mean equalizing E and I, S and N, T and F, or J and P. It means maintaining your preferences and individuality while being able to relate to things previously excluded from them. That direction is different from making all items 50/50.[5]

People have periods for deeply cultivating abilities they are good at, and periods for adapting to society. Turning toward the opposite side is not always correct at any time.

What matters is not a numerical balance but the relationship itself.

Do not make the easy-to-use side and the difficult-to-use side enemies of each other.

## Taking Direction from Jung, Practice Form from Action Research

Jung's ideas provide direction for this book. However, merely citing Jung will not change your actions at tomorrow's meeting.

This book divides two tasks.

From Jung, we receive the direction that consciousness is one-sided and identification with skilled methods makes other possibilities less visible.

From modern action research, we borrow the form of changing abstract personality goals into concrete actions, trying them out small, and comparing predictions with results.

The method in this book is neither Jung's original plan nor something directly proven by experiments in analytical psychology.

It is a bridge to answer Jung's questions within modern life.

## Small Record: Move the Flashlight Once

Recently, choose one recurring difficulty that has happened multiple times and write down two things about it.

### What Is Brightly Illuminated

What are the facts, possibilities, logic, impact on people, schedules, margins, external reactions, and internal sensations that your usual method sees well?

### What Remains in Darkness

From the same list, what is there that has not yet entered judgment?

Finally, pose only one question.

> If the same situation arises again next time, what will you verify once regarding the dark side?

You do not need to become the opposite person.

Simply change the direction of the flashlight slightly, just once.

Even if you cannot illuminate everything at once, merely knowing that there are dark places changes the world you can see.
